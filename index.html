PERSONAL STATEMENTAPPLICATION / 2026

My experiments with AI video have made me want to understand how it is changing creative production. I selected Canva's AI Video Lead role because it connects my interest in AI video with developing an idea into a finished piece. The advertisement's emphasis on visual storytelling, prompt experimentation and production judgement appeals to me (Canva, n.d.-b).

My independent projects have given me a starting point. I have tested local workflows, revised prompts and compared versions before choosing what to present. I enjoy examining an unsatisfactory result and deciding what to change. Course ideas about audiences, communication purpose and reflection have encouraged me to consider what viewers should understand and whether my choices support that purpose.

I see this position as a future career goal and want to become an AI video creator who can interpret a brief, explain creative decisions and deliver consistent work. My next priorities are clearer camera direction and more reliable control over how the model interprets a scene.

Read the original English prompt+

The saved generation prompt, reproduced without rewriting. It describes the intended result.

integrated_multimodal_description: [Shot 1] Photorealistic live-action macro cinematography, a single continuous 8.00-second take. A plain white porcelain espresso cup with one oval handle stands on a worn walnut cafe table in warm morning window light. Dark coffee has a crescent of golden crema. Use the wide perspective of a 24 mm macro probe lens, detailed ceramic glaze, realistic liquid reflections and natural motion blur. The camera follows one unbroken miniature flight path with strong foreground parallax; the environment keeps its scale and geometry. During the first 1.5 seconds, start at tabletop height facing through the empty opening of the cup handle. Accelerate forward through that opening without touching the porcelain, then curve upward outside the cup to clear its rim. As the camera tilts down over the rim, the coffee visibly changes into blue-green seawater and the crema becomes a narrow line of surf; a tiny crescent beach appears along the inside edge. From 1.5 to 5.0 seconds, descend just above the water and race forward into the open hollow of one curling miniature wave. The translucent wave arches overhead like a moving glass tunnel; sunlight flickers through it onto the surface. Keep the bright exit visible ahead, with the cup's immense-looking white inner wall beyond it. The camera clears the open end before the wave folds down behind it, accompanied by a crisp, small surf crash. From 5.0 to 7.2 seconds, continue forward and climb steeply above the rim, then sweep backward and upward in one broad arc while tilting down to keep the cup centred. This rapid physical pull-out reveals that the apparently huge ocean still fits inside the original espresso cup. Ease the camera speed down to a near hover by 7.2 seconds. Finish in a high three-quarter wide view showing the entire cup, its handle and the surrounding tabletop while miniature ripples reach the tiny beach. The cup and table remain rigid and stationary; only the liquid transforms. All seawater stays inside the cup. Camera acceleration creates the speed changes; the wave moves continuously at natural speed. No cuts, hidden transitions, lens zoom, teleportation, camera passing through solid objects, humans, dialogue, text, logos or illustrated rendering.

overall_soundscape: Low cafe room tone continues throughout, with a short close air swish as the camera passes the handle. Delicate stereo surf grows closer during the low flight, with a brief hollow resonance inside the wave and one synchronized miniature splash behind the camera. As the camera rises, the surf recedes smoothly into the quiet room ambience; no voices.

non_diegetic_music: N/A

Learning through AI video

My interest in AI video began while I was browsing videos in my spare time. I wanted to explore how changes in an image could hold a viewer's attention, so I started combining everyday scenes with surreal ideas. My early experiments focused on editing existing content and using first and last frames to guide new sequences. Unfamiliar nodes, models and concepts often led to disappointing results. I taught myself the workflow through small tests with lower generation settings, gradually learning how the different parts worked together.

As I experimented, I noticed that striking visuals and complex camera movements could make the subject harder to follow. I began thinking more carefully about the audience and what I wanted to communicate. I separated each prompt into its subject, environment, action and camera path, then adjusted these elements one at a time. I kept screenshots and production records to compare visual continuity and narrative clarity across versions before choosing the work to present.
